Six in ten customers never came back. The future of this business is 472 people.

2,357 customers · 39 weeks of transactions · 12-month forward view · BG/NBD + Gamma-Gamma, margin-adjusted

The verdict

Projected 12-month customer equity is $103,668 at a 30% gross margin. It is dangerously narrow: the top 20% of customers carry 71% of it (Gini 0.61). Within the high-value tier, 71 customers are statistically fading — purchase patterns say they are likely already gone — taking $5,393 (5%) of future value with them. That group, not the 60% of one-and-done buyers, is where the next retention euro earns its highest return.

Recommended actions, in order

  1. Reallocate before spending more. Suppress the 330 statistically-dead accounts from paid remarketing and redirect that budget to the 71 fading high-value accounts. Same spend, opposite expected return.
  2. Treat the first repeat purchase as the conversion that matters. With 60% one-and-done, moving second-purchase conversion by even a few points shifts more equity than any acquisition campaign of equal cost. The frequency–recency matrix gives the timing window.
  3. Put a number on concentration risk in the next board pack. Top-10% share is 57%; report it quarterly like a covenant. If it rises while equity is flat, the base is hollowing out beneath a stable top line.

Methodology gate — what was checked before you were shown a single number

PASS
Business structure: non-contractual & continuous

Retail music purchases; attrition unobserved -> BTYD family is the valid model class. (A contractual log would gate to hazard/retention models.)

PASS
Calibration depth: repeat-purchase mass

40% of 2,357 customers have >=1 repeat purchase in 39 weeks. BTYD needs enough repeaters to identify the dropout process.

PASS
Monetary integrity

0 repeat customers with non-positive average spend (refund/freebie contamination would corrupt Gamma-Gamma).

FLAG
Gamma-Gamma independence (frequency ⟂ monetary)

Pearson r = +0.114, Spearman rho = +0.206 (p=0.000). Positive dependence means frequent buyers also spend more per order, so Gamma-Gamma UNDERSTATES the value of heavy buyers: reported CLV concentration is a conservative floor, not a ceiling. Proceeding under this caveat; v0.2 substitutes a joint frequency-spend model.

FLAG
In-sample purchase-count reconciliation

Model-implied total repeat purchases within 17.4% of observed. (v0.2 adds true calibration/holdout split.)
